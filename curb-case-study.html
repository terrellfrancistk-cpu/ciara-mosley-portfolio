Case study 01 / TK Hub · Product UX

Show the fare before the ride.

Curb puts the total and the breakdown in front of the rider while there’s still a decision to make.

The design decision

Put the total first.

A price is easier to trust when someone can see what it includes. Curb’s public estimator leads with what the rider pays and gives the breakdown its own space.

  1. Start with what you pay.

    The sample total of $31.33 is visible before the breakdown opens.

  2. Separate the fare and the fee.

    The $28.48 fare and $2.85 flat fee add up to the displayed total for this example.

  3. Explain the tax line.

    The estimator says tax is inside the fare, so the $0.00 line shows that no tax is added on top in this example.

The question
Can a rider tell what they’ll pay and what’s included before they decide to request the trip?
My role
As TK Hub’s co-founder and COO, I review Curb’s user experience and launch readiness, document issues, and turn findings into priorities.
What I look for
A clear total, a breakdown that explains the fee, and wording a rider can understand without opening a help article.

Public ride demo

See the price change as you choose.

In Curb’s sample app, I select Sundance Square and switch between Saver and Standard. The quoted price updates as the ride option changes, before any request is made.
